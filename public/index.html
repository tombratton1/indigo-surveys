Different homes need different levels of inspection. Tell us about the property and we’ll help you choose.

02 LEVEL TWO

Homebuyer survey

A practical overview for a conventional property that appears to be in reasonable condition.

  • Assessment of visible property condition
  • Highlights significant defects and concerns
  • Advice on further checks where needed

Often suited to
Conventional homes without extensive alterations.

03 LEVEL THREE

Building survey

A more detailed assessment for older, larger, altered or unusual properties, or homes needing significant work.

  • More detailed inspection and condition analysis
  • Explains defects and their likely causes
  • Advice on repairs and maintenance

A FEW HELPFUL ANSWERS

Buying a home?
Start with clarity.

Ask us about your property ↗
Is a mortgage valuation the same as a survey?

No. A lender’s valuation is primarily for the lender’s lending decision. A home survey considers the property’s condition for you and can identify defects and further investigations.

Do I need a Level 2 or Level 3 survey?

Level 2 is generally suitable for conventional homes in reasonable condition. Level 3 provides more detail for older, altered, unusual or run-down properties. The surveyor should confirm the right level for your particular home.

When should I arrange a survey?

Buyers commonly arrange a survey after an offer is accepted and before becoming legally committed to the purchase. Allow time to review the report and investigate any concerns.

How much does a survey cost?

The price depends on the property, its size, location and the survey level. Request a quote for the property you’re considering; fees and any VAT will be explained before instruction.

Can a survey identify every problem?

No. Surveys have agreed limits and are generally based on accessible, visible areas. Concealed defects may not be identified, and specialist checks may be recommended. Your surveyor will explain the scope and exclusions.
